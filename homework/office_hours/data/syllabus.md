# AGNT 6100: Building Agentic Systems — Spring 2027 Syllabus

This is a synthetic syllabus for a fictional course, written for the Office Hours Bot homework.
Every name, date and policy below is made up.

## Course overview
AGNT 6100 teaches students to design, build, evaluate and deploy LLM agents. Topics include
retrieval-augmented generation, tool use, LangGraph state machines, multi-agent supervisors,
human-in-the-loop approval and production observability. The course is 3 credits and meets in
person on Tuesdays and Thursdays, 2:00–3:20 pm, in Room 417 of the Lin Engineering Building.

## Instructor and teaching assistants
The instructor is Prof. Mira Okafor (mira.okafor@agnt6100.example.edu). The teaching assistants
are Daniel Reyes (daniel.reyes@agnt6100.example.edu), who handles homework grading and
regrades, and Priya Natarajan (priya.natarajan@agnt6100.example.edu), who handles the final
project and extension requests. Email the TA who owns the topic; the course inbox
(staff@agnt6100.example.edu) reaches everyone.

## Office hours
Prof. Okafor holds office hours on Wednesdays, 4:00–5:30 pm, in Room 512 of the Lin
Engineering Building. Daniel Reyes holds office hours on Mondays, 11:00 am–12:30 pm, on Zoom
(link pinned in the course forum). Priya Natarajan holds office hours on Fridays, 1:00–2:30 pm,
in the 4th-floor study lounge. No office hours are held during spring break.

## Prerequisites
Students must have completed Intro to Machine Learning (ML 5010) or an equivalent course, and be
comfortable writing Python 3. Familiarity with REST APIs and Git is expected. There is no
prerequisite in natural language processing.

## Textbook and materials
There is no required textbook. Readings are posted on the course site each week. Students need a
laptop and an LLM API key; the department provides each student with a USD 40 API credit, which
is enough for every homework if you cache embeddings and avoid re-running whole evaluations.

## Grading breakdown
Final grades are computed as follows: homework assignments 30% (five homeworks, 6% each),
midterm exam 20%, final project 35%, participation and weekly quizzes 15%. There is no final
exam. Letter grades use these cutoffs: A at 93, A- at 90, B+ at 87, B at 83, B- at 80, C+ at 77,
C at 70. Grades are not curved.

## Homework submission
Homeworks are submitted on Gradescope by 11:59 pm Eastern Time on the due date. Each submission
must include the code, a README, and the evaluation results file. Late submissions are handled
under the late policy below.

## Late policy and slip days
Every student has 3 slip days for the semester, which can be used on homeworks only, not on the
midterm or final project. A slip day extends a homework deadline by 24 hours with no penalty, and
you do not need to ask for one; Gradescope tracks them automatically. Once your slip days are
used up, late homework loses 10% per day and is not accepted more than 3 days late.

## Extensions
Extensions beyond slip days are granted only for documented emergencies such as illness, family
emergencies or religious observance. Request an extension by emailing Priya Natarajan before the
deadline, with documentation if available. Extensions are not granted for job interviews,
travel, or workload from other courses. The TA, not the Office Hours Bot, decides every request.

## Midterm exam
The midterm exam is on Thursday, March 11, 2027, during class time (2:00–3:20 pm) in Room 417.
It is closed-book, but you may bring one double-sided letter-size page of handwritten notes. The
midterm covers weeks 1 through 6. Missing the midterm without a documented emergency results in
a zero; with documentation, a make-up exam is scheduled within one week.

## Final project
The final project is completed in teams of 3 to 4 students. The proposal is due Friday,
March 26, 2027; the checkpoint demo is in class on Thursday, April 15, 2027; and the final report
and code are due Friday, May 7, 2027. Final presentations are held on Tuesday, May 4, and
Thursday, May 6, 2027. Within the 35%, the proposal is worth 5%, the checkpoint 10%, and the
final report, code and presentation together 20%.

## Weekly quizzes and participation
A short quiz opens on the course site every Monday at 9:00 am and closes Wednesday at 11:59 pm.
Each quiz has 5 questions and takes about 10 minutes. The lowest two quiz scores are dropped.
Participation is earned by attending class and posting in the course forum.

## Regrade requests
Regrade requests are submitted through Gradescope within 7 days of grades being released. Explain
which rubric item you believe was misapplied. Daniel Reyes reviews homework regrades; Prof.
Okafor reviews midterm regrades. A regrade may raise or lower your score.

## Collaboration policy
You may discuss homework ideas with classmates, but code and write-ups must be your own. List the
names of anyone you discussed a homework with at the top of your README. Sharing code or copying
from another student is a violation of academic integrity and is reported to the dean's office.

## AI tool policy
You may use AI coding assistants on homeworks and the final project, but you must disclose which
tools you used and for what in your README. AI tools are not allowed during the midterm exam.
You are responsible for every line you submit, including code an assistant wrote.

## Attendance
Attendance is expected but not recorded for grading beyond participation. If you miss a class,
the lecture recording is posted to the course site within 24 hours. You do not need to email
the staff to say you will miss a regular class.

## Accommodations
Students who need exam or assignment accommodations should register with the university's
Disability Services office and share their accommodation letter with Prof. Okafor at least one
week before the midterm. Accommodations cannot be applied retroactively.

## Communication
Questions about course material go in the course forum, where answers help everyone. Private or
personal matters go by email to the relevant TA. Staff aim to reply within 2 business days.
Announcements are posted on the course site and sent by email.

## Weekly schedule
Week 1 (Jan 26): LLMs, tokens and inference cost. Week 2 (Feb 2): keyword and vector search.
Week 3 (Feb 9): hybrid search and re-ranking. Week 4 (Feb 16): RAG and chunking. Week 5
(Feb 23): agents and tool calling. Week 6 (Mar 2): LangGraph state, nodes and edges. Week 7
(Mar 9): midterm review and the midterm on March 11. Spring break is March 15–19, with no class.
Week 8 (Mar 23): agentic RAG. Week 9 (Mar 30): multi-agent supervisors. Week 10 (Apr 6):
human-in-the-loop and checkpointing. Week 11 (Apr 13): evaluation and the checkpoint demo.
Week 12 (Apr 20): security and prompt injection. Week 13 (Apr 27): deployment and observability.
Week 14 (May 4): final presentations.

## Homework due dates
Homework 1 is due Friday, February 12, 2027. Homework 2 is due Friday, February 26, 2027.
Homework 3 is due Friday, March 26, 2027. Homework 4 is due Friday, April 9, 2027. Homework 5,
the Office Hours Bot, is due Friday, April 23, 2027.
